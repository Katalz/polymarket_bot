# Why "True Gabagool" is Impossible for $140

## The Mathematical Impossibility

The original Gabagool strategy is a **Market Making & Hedging** strategy. It relies on being able to buy and sell small fractions of a position to manage risk.

### 1. The "Pixel Problem" (Granularity)
*   **Polymarket Constraint:** Minimum ticket size is **~$5.00**. This is the smallest "pixel" of trade.
*   **The Gabagool Move:** "I have a $1,000 position. The price moved up. I will sell $50 (5%) to lock in profit." -> **POSSIBLE**.
*   **The Problem for You:** You have a $15 position (3 tickets).
    *   You cannot sell 5%. The smallest you can sell is $5 (33%).
    *   **Result:** You cannot hedge finely. You are forced to be "All-in" or "All-out".

### 2. The "Bankroll Barrier"
*   **To Hedge Properly:** You need a position size large enough that $5 represents a small adjustment (e.g., 5-10%).
    *   Minimum Position Size = $50 - $100.
*   **Risk Management Rule:** Never put more than 5% of your bankroll on one trade.
    *   Required Bankroll = $100 / 0.05 = **$2,000**.
*   **Reality:** With **$140**, "Proper Hedging" is mathematically impossible without risking ruin (putting >50% of bankroll on one match).

## The "Spread Trap" (Why Hedging Kills Small Accounts)

Hedging isn't free. You pay the **Bid-Ask Spread** (the difference between Buy and Sell price) every time you trade.

*   **Scenario:** You buy YES.
*   **Gabagool (Maker):** He places limit orders and earns the spread.
*   **You (Taker):** You cross the spread to buy ($5 ticket).
    *   Cost = ~2-5% (Implicit Fee).
*   **If you Hedge (Buy NO):** You pay the spread *again*.
    *   Total Cost = ~5-10%.
*   **Result:** On a small account, hedging costs more than the potential profit. You are **guaranteed to lose money** slowly by paying spread fees.
